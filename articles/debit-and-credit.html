Accounting Basics: Debit and Credit Explained

Debit and credit are the two sides of double-entry bookkeeping. Each transaction is recorded so that total debits equal total credits.

Basic account rules

  • Assets: increase with a debit and decrease with a credit.
  • Expenses: increase with a debit and decrease with a credit.
  • Liabilities: increase with a credit and decrease with a debit.
  • Capital and income: generally increase with a credit and decrease with a debit.

Example: stationery purchased for cash

A business purchases stationery worth ₹1,000 in cash. Debit Stationery Expense ₹1,000 because the expense increases. Credit Cash ₹1,000 because cash decreases.

Remember: total debit must equal total credit in a balanced journal entry.

Why this matters

These rules help you understand journal entries, review ledger postings and investigate differences. Always consider the real transaction and your organisation's accounting policy.
