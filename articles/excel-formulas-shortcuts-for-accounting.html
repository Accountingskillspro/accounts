Excel Formulas and Shortcut Keys Every Accountant Should Know

Excel is widely used in accounting for reconciliations, expense analysis, invoice checking, ageing reports, GST data review and month-end reporting. This practical guide explains common formulas and keyboard shortcuts with examples you can try in a worksheet.

Practice tip: Create a copy of your working file before testing formulas. Examples below use commas between formula arguments; depending on your Excel regional settings, you may need semicolons instead.

1. Sample accounting data

Imagine a worksheet with these columns: A = Vendor, B = Invoice No., C = Invoice Date, D = Amount, E = Due Date, F = Paid Amount. Enter sample transactions from row 2 onwards. The examples below use these columns unless stated otherwise.

2. Essential Excel formulas for accounting

SUM — total expenses, invoices or payments

=SUM(D2:D100)

Adds all numeric values in D2 through D100. Use it to total invoices, expenses, receipts or payments.

SUMIF — total for one condition

=SUMIF(A2:A100,"ABC Traders",D2:D100)

Adds invoice amounts for ABC Traders. Useful for a vendor-wise expense total.

SUMIFS — total with multiple conditions

=SUMIFS(D2:D100,A2:A100,"ABC Traders",C2:C100,">="&DATE(2026,10,1),C2:C100,"<"&DATE(2026,11,1))

Totals invoices for ABC Traders dated in October 2026. Useful for vendor- and month-wise reporting. Update the dates for your reporting period.

IF — show a result based on a condition

=IF(D2=F2,"Paid","Check")

Shows Paid when invoice amount equals paid amount; otherwise shows Check. In real files, account for partial payments and credit notes according to your process.

IFERROR — handle formula errors neatly

=IFERROR(D2/F2,0)

Returns 0 instead of an error if the division fails. Use an appropriate message or blank instead of 0 when a zero could be mistaken for a real accounting value.

COUNTIF and COUNTIFS — count invoices or duplicates

=COUNTIF(B2:B100,B2)

Counts how often the invoice number in B2 appears in the range. A result greater than 1 can flag a possible duplicate; verify vendor, date, amount and source documents before concluding it is a duplicate.

=COUNTIFS(A2:A100,A2,B2:B100,B2)

Counts rows where both vendor and invoice number match.

XLOOKUP — retrieve matching information

=XLOOKUP(B2,InvoiceRegister!B:B,InvoiceRegister!D:D,"Not found")

Looks for the invoice number in column B of InvoiceRegister and returns the corresponding amount from column D. XLOOKUP is available in newer Excel versions; use VLOOKUP or INDEX/MATCH if your version does not support it.

VLOOKUP — lookup for older Excel versions

=IFERROR(VLOOKUP(B2,InvoiceRegister!B:D,3,FALSE),"Not found")

Searches for B2 in the first column of B:D and returns the third column of that selected range. FALSE requests an exact match.

ROUND — round calculated amounts

=ROUND(D2*18%,2)

Calculates 18% of the amount in D2 and rounds the result to two decimal places. Use the actual applicable tax rate and check tax rules before posting GST entries.

SUBTOTAL — total filtered records

=SUBTOTAL(9,D2:D100)

Returns a sum that responds to filtered rows. Handy for reviewing selected vendors or expense categories after applying filters.

TEXT — display dates in a readable format

=TEXT(C2,"dd-mmm-yyyy")

Displays a date like 11-Oct-2026. Use this for presentation only; keep actual date values for date calculations and sorting.

3. Practical accounting formulas

Outstanding amount

If D is invoice amount and F is paid amount: =D2-F2. Confirm how credit notes, withholding and adjustments are handled in your ledger.

Days overdue

If E is the due date and G is outstanding amount: =IF(G2>0,MAX(0,TODAY()-E2),0). This returns zero for invoices not outstanding or not yet overdue. Use your organisation's ageing policy for reporting.

Ageing bucket

For days overdue in H2: =IF(G2=0,"Settled",IF(H2<=30,"1-30 days",IF(H2<=60,"31-60 days",IF(H2<=90,"61-90 days","90+ days")))). Adjust buckets to match your company's policy.

Bank reconciliation difference

If adjusted book balance is B2 and adjusted bank balance is C2: =B2-C2. A zero difference indicates the two adjusted balances agree, but unmatched entries still need investigation.

3-way matching quantity check

If PO quantity is E2, GRN received quantity is F2 and invoice quantity is G2: =IF(AND(E2=F2,F2=G2),"MATCH","INVESTIGATE"). In production files, use agreed tolerances and investigate rate, amount, tax and document differences too. See our 3-way matching guide.

5. How to use formulas safely in accounting

  1. Keep a protected copy of the original export or source file.
  2. Convert clean transaction data into an Excel Table with Ctrl + T.
  3. Use unique references and exact-match lookups wherever possible.
  4. Check formulas on a few known transactions before filling them down.
  5. Reconcile totals to the source ledger or report and investigate exceptions.
  6. Document assumptions, tolerances and manual adjustments.

Conclusion

Start with SUM, SUMIF, SUMIFS, IF, COUNTIF and lookup formulas, then practise shortcuts on a copy of real-world style accounting data. Excel supports analysis and review, but it does not replace source-document checks, accounting judgement or your organisation's approval controls.
