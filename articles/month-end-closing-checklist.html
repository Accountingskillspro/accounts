PRACTICAL ACCOUNTING • MONTH-END CLOSE

Month-End Closing Checklist for Accountants

A consistent month-end close helps the accounts team spot missing entries, investigate differences and prepare more reliable financial reports. Adapt this checklist to your company’s accounting policy, materiality and approval process.

Suggested approach: Assign an owner and due date to each task, keep evidence of review, and maintain a list of open issues rather than silently carrying differences forward.

1. Sales and revenue

  • Check sales invoices, credit notes and cut-off around month-end.
  • Review dispatch, delivery and billing reports for unbilled or duplicate transactions.
  • Reconcile sales register totals with the general ledger and relevant tax reports.

2. Purchases and accounts payable

  • Record supplier invoices received before the close cut-off.
  • Review goods received but not invoiced and invoices pending GRN or approval.
  • Perform vendor ledger reconciliation for key suppliers and investigate old debit/credit balances.
  • Complete PO–GRN–invoice three-way matching where applicable.

3. Bank, cash and payments

  • Prepare bank reconciliation for each bank account.
  • Investigate unreconciled entries, bank charges, interest and payments in transit.
  • Reconcile cash book balances and check unusual or negative cash balances.
  • Review payment runs, rejected payments and advances awaiting adjustment.

4. Payroll, expenses and accruals

  • Post approved payroll, reimbursements and statutory payroll entries.
  • Identify expenses relating to the month where the supplier invoice has not yet arrived.
  • Review prepaid expenses and release the appropriate monthly portion.
  • Document accrual calculations and reverse them in the following period when appropriate.

5. Inventory and fixed assets

  • Reconcile inventory records to the general ledger and investigate stock adjustments.
  • Review goods in transit, material consumption and unusual purchase price or quantity variances.
  • Record approved asset additions, disposals and depreciation.
  • Check capital versus revenue classification for significant purchases.

6. GST and statutory checks

  • Reconcile sales and purchase registers with the relevant GST reports.
  • Review invoice details, credit notes, reverse-charge cases and blocked or ineligible ITC considerations.
  • Compare eligible ITC information with GSTR-2B and investigate differences before taking action.
  • Confirm filing requirements and due dates from current official guidance.

GST credit should not be claimed solely because a line appears in an auto-drafted statement; eligibility and applicable conditions still need to be checked. Refer to the official GST portal guidance on GSTR-2B.

7. General ledger review and reporting

  • Review suspense, clearing, advances and intercompany accounts.
  • Check trial balance movements and investigate unusual balances or month-on-month fluctuations.
  • Reconcile sub-ledgers to control accounts, including vendors, customers, inventory and fixed assets.
  • Prepare profit and loss, balance sheet and cash-flow information required by management.
  • Obtain reviewer approval and lock the period according to company policy.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Posting a journal without supporting documents or approval.
  • Ignoring old reconciliation differences month after month.
  • Changing a prior period without following the close-control process.
  • Assuming a formula or system report proves the underlying transaction is correct.
Remember: A good close is not just finishing entries quickly. It is completing, reconciling, reviewing and documenting them so that the reported numbers can be explained.
